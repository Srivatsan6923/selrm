\title{Learning When to Change: Symbolic Supervision for\\
Selective Evidence Sensitivity in Medical Reward Models}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reward models decide which medical reasoning traces are kept, reranked and
reinforced. A useful reward reverses its preference when decisive patient
evidence changes and holds it when similar-looking evidence does not apply: a
penicillin allergy in the patient is a reason to change an order, the same
allergy in the patient's mother is not. We test this \emph{selective
sensitivity} with symbolic triplets from executable, stated clinical rules: a
base case, a flip of one decisive input, and a near-miss that mentions the
same concept without making it applicable. On rules of held-out structure,
released medical PRMs solve \res{min/run/C-AUD-meds3/L2/all/TA|run/C-AUD-medprm/L2/all/TA@0}--\res{max/run/C-AUD-meds3/L2/all/TA|run/C-AUD-medprm/L2/all/TA@0}\% of triplets. Trained on decisive
edits alone, a verifier reverses (\res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/L2/all/Rev/min@0}--\res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/L2/all/Rev/max@0}\%) and does not hold (\res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/L2/all/Hold/min@0}--\res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/L2/all/Hold/max@0}\%);
near-misses in the same budget raise its triplet accuracy from \res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/L2/all/TA}\% to
\res{run/B-F-verdict-triplets/L2/all/TA}\%, and a separately written evidence record to \res{run/B-F-summary2-triplets/L2/all/TA}--\res{run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/L2/all/TA}\%, a gain
that a single applicability bit carries. The trained verifiers follow edits
to the rule itself, but where no rule is stated they do not transfer: on
MedEinst and on exam-question pairs they are level with or below the
untrained backbone. Selectivity is relative to a criterion. We therefore
make the criterion a sourced input and execute the parts of applicability
that are executable (\ph{gate not yet run}). On clinical notes with a
published score definition near-misses help again ($+\res{cmp/p7/diff}$ points on
criterion claims, $+\res{cmp/p9/diff}$ on edited notes), but not demonstrably by
reading the criterion. A policy optimised against a released medical PRM that
fails the test drives its reward to \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/step=1000/reward/s0@2} while its accuracy falls from
\res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/start/pair/s0}\% to \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/final/pair/s0}\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A reward model for medical reasoning is asked whether a step or an answer is
right for the patient at hand. Often the answer turns on one line of the case.
Under a rule that orders amoxicillin for streptococcal pharyngitis unless the
patient is allergic to penicillin, the order is right for a patient with no
drug allergy and wrong for one with a documented penicillin allergy
(Figure~\ref{fig:example}). It is right again for a patient whose mother has
that allergy. A reward that prefers the same order in all three cases fails to
reverse when required; one that changes its preference in the second and the
third reverses under an edit that leaves the decision unchanged. Whether
evidence applies also depends on the rule: a stroke ten years ago counts
under a score that asks for any prior stroke and not under a criterion that
asks about the last six months.

Both failures matter because reward models filter training traces
\citep{chen2024huatuo}, rerank candidates \citep{snell2025scaling,yun2025medprm}
and supply the reward in reinforcement learning
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025deepseekr1}, where a learned reward that
can be satisfied without solving the task is exploited
\citep{gao2023scaling,tiwari2026reward}. The first failure is documented:
the mean accuracy of 28 LLMs on guideline-derived medication questions falls
from 63.6\% to 45.1\% when a patient fact changes the answer
\citep{hou2026medpic}. The second is easy to overlook: a change after a
targeted edit means little unless it exceeds the change after an edit that
should not matter \citep{yang2026compared,chen2026judge}. Medical reward
models are evaluated on answer selection and injected step errors
\citep{yun2025medprm,wu2026medprmbench}, neither of which changes the case.
What is not established is what a reward learns when the ``irrelevant''
evidence concerns the very concept the rule tests, which supervision teaches
it when that evidence applies, and what it needs where no rule is written
down.

We build the test from executable rules (\S\ref{sec:twins}). Each rule yields
\emph{triplets}: a base case, a \emph{decisive edit} (flip) of one input, and
a \emph{near-miss} in which the decisive concept appears without counting
under the rule: in a relative, negated, at the wrong time, or as a value
that approaches a threshold without crossing it. The rule program
labels every case against the two claims the rule can support. Labels state
what a stated rule implies, not what is clinically best. A \emph{claim
verifier} scores a claim against a case, a \emph{process reward model} ranks
traces with such scores, and a \emph{training reward} is optimised against by
a policy; a result at one level is not evidence for the next. Our
contributions:

\noindent\textbf{(1) A test.} Triplets separate two failures that pair tests
conflate, and rule-side edits change whether the same evidence counts while
the case stays fixed. On rules of held-out structure, two released medical
PRMs solve \res{run/C-AUD-meds3/L2/all/TA}\% and \res{run/C-AUD-medprm/L2/all/TA}\% of triplets and the untrained backbone \res{run/C-TF-critic/L2/all/TA}\%
(\S\ref{sec:results-audit}).

\noindent\textbf{(2) A controlled comparison} of training distributions and
representations under one budget (\S\ref{sec:results-factorial}). Every
corpus without near-misses yields reversal without holding. Near-misses raise
the triplet accuracy of a verdict-only verifier from \res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/L2/all/TA}\% to \res{run/B-F-verdict-triplets/L2/all/TA}\%, and a
record written in a separate pass to \res{run/B-F-summary2-triplets/L2/all/TA}--\res{run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/L2/all/TA}\%. A reader that writes one
applicability bit matches the full record: what is learned is the
applicability decision, not a schema.

\noindent\textbf{(3) A boundary, and a design that follows from it.} The
trained verifiers follow edits to the applicability clause of the rule
(\res{run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/xr_v1:test/xa/XA/min@0}--\res{run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/xr_v1:test/xa/XA/max@0}\% crossed accuracy, against \res{run/C-TF-critic/xr_v1:test/top/XA@0}\% untrained) and do not transfer where
the criterion is implicit (\S\ref{sec:results-shift}): selectivity is
relative to a criterion. We treat applicability as a conjunction of five
typed conditions, each checked by an open symbolic source where one exists,
and make the criterion a sourced input (\emph{criterion grounding},
\S\ref{sec:ledger}), which we evaluate on clinical notes with stated score
definitions: near-misses help there too, but not demonstrably by reading the
criterion (\S\ref{sec:results-ground}).

\noindent\textbf{(4) Reward use.} On exam questions, which state no
criterion, the verifier's score carries almost no signal. As a training
reward, a released medical PRM that fails the triplet test is exploited: its
reward reaches \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/step=1000/reward/s0@2} while accuracy on held-out rules falls from \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/start/pair/s0}\% to
\res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/final/pair/s0}\% (\S\ref{sec:results-downstream}).

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Evaluator validity and contrastive supervision.}
A valid judge is invariant to construct-preserving edits and sensitive to
construct-changing ones \citep{chen2026judge,weng2026policy,yang2026compared}.
Clinical tests change a patient fact that decides the answer
\citep{hou2026medpic,chen2026medeinst,kim2025marc} or add clinically
irrelevant distractors \citep{vishwanath2025distracted}; none pairs a
decisive edit with an inapplicable mention of the same concept, edits the
applicability clause of a rule, or is applied to medical PRMs.
Label-flipping minimal edits \citep{kaushik2020cad,gardner2020contrast} teach
which feature matters and not when it does not \citep{joshi2022cad}; adding
label-preserving edits removes over-triggering for negation scope
\citep{she2023scone} and safety policies \citep{liu2026apt}, and reward
models are trained with causal and neutral augmentations
\citep{srivastava2026crome,liu2026dynacf}, whereas clinical post-training on
guideline functions keeps only outcome-changing interventions
\citep{shen2026medguidex}. Our negatives keep the decisive concept and
remove one condition of its applicability, a near miss in the sense of
\citet{winston1970}.

\paragraph{Symbolic verification, stated rules and sources of criteria.}
PRMs are trained on step labels, rollouts or generated verification
\citep{lightman2024verify,wang2024mathshepherd,khalifa2025thinkprm,zhao2025genprm},
or on labels from formal tools \citep{kamoi2026fover}. NS-PRM lets a
deterministic verifier decide what is executable and trains the PRM on
negatives that pass it \citep{zi2026nsprm}; all its perturbations change the
label, and we add the label-preserving side. Medical verifiers condition
on retrieved passages \citep{yun2025medprm,zhang2026medtiv}; scoring from a
record written in a separate pass follows \citet{dhuliawala2024cove} and
\citet{evpv2026}, with the assertion attributes of clinical NLP as fields
\citep{chapman2001negex,harkema2009context}. Rule-conditioned reasoning has
many benchmarks \citep{saeidi2018sharc,holzenberger2020sara,clark2020ruletaker,sun2024rulebench},
yet deleting or replacing the rule left every compliance detector audited by
\citet{sadhu2026compliance} unchanged. Clinical criteria are executed as guideline functions
\citep{cao2026guideskill}, checked as disease profiles \citep{cao2026veridx}
or compiled for a solver \citep{zhou2026verdict}, with prompted readers.
Ontologies supply exactly labelled subsumption data with sibling negatives
\citep{he2023ontolama,he2024hit,qu2026medieval}; injected into weights they
changed medical QA little \citep{liu2025ontotune}, while a type vocabulary
used as a gate before a learned judge helped \citep{shan2026ontgqa}. Learned
rewards are exploited under optimisation
\citep{skalse2022hacking,mahmoud2026hacking,pronesti2026vprm}
(Appendix~\ref{app:related}).

\section{Rule Triplets, Criterion Sources and External Tiers}
\label{sec:twins}

\subsection{Rule tier}

\paragraph{Rules and applicability.}
A rule $\rho$ has criteria $c_1,\dots,c_m$ over typed inputs. A case is
rendered from a state, a set of \emph{mentions}, each with a concept, a
value, a subject (patient or other), a status (present, absent, unknown) and
a time (current or past). Every criterion $c_j$ comes with an
\emph{applicability predicate} that says which mentions it counts, a
conjunction over the five attributes $\mathcal{K}=\{$concept, subject,
status, time, value$\}$:
\begin{equation}
A_j(m)=\bigwedge_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\bigl[m_k\in D_j^{k}\bigr],
\label{eq:applic}
\end{equation}
where $D_j^{k}$ is the set of values of attribute $k$ that $c_j$ accepts. The
predicate is part of the rule: a creatinine threshold counts the patient's
current value, a stroke-risk score a stroke at any time. In \res{stats/library_semantics.criteria_past_or_family_pct}\% of
criteria a past or family finding counts, so no global heuristic about time
or subject is correct. In the frozen library the concept condition is an
identity test. Rules are clinical scores or \emph{constraint rules} (choose
$a$ unless $c$ holds, then $b$). Each group is scored on \emph{conclusion
claims}, the two outcomes the rule can support in neutral wording, and on
\emph{criterion claims} (``criterion $j$ is met''). The prompt states the
rule.

\paragraph{Edits.}
The engine edits one input of a criterion and keeps an edit only if
executing the program confirms its invariant on the target claim. A
\emph{flip} changes a counted mention so that the claim changes. A
\emph{near-miss} reports the concept and value of the flip in a mention for
which exactly one condition of Eq.~\ref{eq:applic} is false, and leaves the
claim unchanged: another \emph{subject}, an absent \emph{status}, a
\emph{time} the predicate does not count, or a \emph{value} that moves
toward the threshold without crossing it (exactly at the threshold:
\emph{boundary}). Presentation edits re-render the same state, and
missing twins replace a decisive input by an explicit unknown
(Appendix~\ref{app:twins}). \emph{Rule-side items} keep the case and change
one applicability clause of the rule (``ever'' against ``within the last six
months''; the patient against the patient or a first-degree relative). An
item crosses two rules with three cases and is solved only if all six
judgments are right (crossed accuracy, XA); the \res{json/data/xr_v1/test/MANIFEST.json::n_items} items were built after
the library was frozen, and no system is trained on them.

\paragraph{Triplets and metrics.}
A triplet crosses a base case $x$, its flip $\xf$ and a near-miss $\xn$ with
the claims $s$ (correct for $x$ and $\xn$) and $s'$ (correct for $\xf$). For a
reward with score $u(s,x)$ let $d(x)=u(s,x)-u(s',x)$. The triplet is
\emph{solved} if $d(x)>0$, $d(\xf)<0$ and $d(\xn)>0$; ties are failures.
\emph{Reversal} (Rev) is the share of base--flip pairs with $d(x)>0$ and
$d(\xf)<0$, \emph{Hold} the share of near-misses with $d(\xn)>0$, and
\emph{triplet accuracy} (TA) the share of solved triplets, the primary
summary; TA$_{c}$ is TA on criterion claims. Three fair preference signs
solve a triplet with probability $1/8$, so TA is read with base accuracy. A
deterministic scorer that sees only the claim, only the case, or only
whether the decisive concept is named solves none
(Appendix~\ref{app:twins}).

\paragraph{Criterion stated by the dataset.}
\emph{\mcv} is the rule-based subset of MedCalc-Bench
\citep{khandekar2024medcalc} in its maintained release, at a tag that
postdates two independent audits \citep{ye2025stewardship,krohn2026audit}:
19 clinical scores and 380 test notes, 230 of them human-written case
reports and vignettes. Each instance ships the text of the score and the
value of every criterion input, so criterion-level labels follow from
released values by rule code. In the second audit, appending the calculator
specification to the prompt raised one model's answer accuracy from 51.9\%
to 81.5\%, the effect our premise predicts for a verifier. \emph{Registered criteria}
are single-condition eligibility criteria quoted from the registry, whose
operator, value, unit, time window and negation come from published
annotated corpora \citep{kury2020chia,dobbins2022lct}. The \emph{TrialGPT
annotations} \citep{jin2024trialgpt} give physician judgments for 1{,}015
patient--criterion pairs; in only \res{sum2/json/results_git/C-TG-critic/summary_clin_v1~trialgpt_test.json::per_class_five_names.not included.n|json/results_git/C-TG-critic/summary_clin_v1~trialgpt_test.json::per_class_five_names.excluded.n|json/results_git/C-TG-critic-dev/summary_clin_v1~trialgpt_dev.json::per_class_five_names.not included.n|json/results_git/C-TG-critic-dev/summary_clin_v1~trialgpt_dev.json::per_class_five_names.excluded.n@0} does the label make the patient
ineligible.

\paragraph{Criterion derived from an open source.}
The cases of \emph{MedEinst} \citep{chen2026medeinst} are narrated from
DDXPlus patients \citep{tchango2022ddxplus}, whose released knowledge base
lists which of 223 findings are associated with each of 49 conditions. For a
pair of diagnoses a frozen script renders the findings listed for one only,
the other only and both, with the one procedure the lists support (exclusion
by a finding listed for the other diagnosis only), identically for both
members of a pair. Class-level terms (``an ACE inhibitor'') take their
membership from open ontologies: drug classes from RxNorm ingredients and
RxClass \citep{nelson2011rxnorm}, phenotypes from HPO \citep{gargano2024hpo},
diseases from Mondo \citep{vasilevsky2022mondo}; none requires a SNOMED CT or
UMLS licence. There the flip names a member and the near-miss a relative
outside the closure of the class (Figure~\ref{fig:example}): the concept
condition of Eq.~\ref{eq:applic}, which the frozen library never varies.

\paragraph{No criterion.}
\emph{Key pairs} are two exam questions that share answer options and differ
in the keyed answer, from MedQA \citep{jin2021medqa} and CareQA
\citep{ariasduart2025careqa}; what decides between the answers is knowledge
that no input states. \emph{NLI4CT-P} \citep{jullien2024semeval} edits
statements about trial reports (Appendix~\ref{app:qa}). These tiers are the
boundary of the method, not its target.

\section{\method}
\label{sec:ledger}

\paragraph{Reader.}
The reader $R_\phi$ receives the case $x$, the criterion text $\rho$, and the
\emph{condition under test} $\omega$: the criterion, or the set of candidate
answers, on which the claims disagree. It does not receive a candidate's
asserted outcome, so the claims of a triplet are judged from one ledger
$e=R_\phi(x,\rho,\omega)$. An entry records facts and no decision: the
condition the claim depends on (\texttt{need}), what the case says about it
(\texttt{found}: a value, a verbatim quotation, or \texttt{missing}), and
the \texttt{subject}, \texttt{status} and \texttt{time} of the finding. A
quotation that does not occur in the case makes the ledger malformed. On the
rule tier $\omega$ is supplied; an ablation has the reader find it.

\paragraph{Judge.}
The judge $J_\phi$ receives the ledger, the criterion text and the claim:
\begin{equation}
u(s,x)=J_\phi\bigl(R_\phi(x,\rho,\omega),\rho,s\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $J_\phi(e,\rho,s)=\log p_\phi(+\mid e,\rho,s)-\log p_\phi(-\mid
e,\rho,s)$. The case reaches the verdict only through the ledger. A malformed
ledger rejects both claims, and the tie counts as a failure. Reader and
judge are two prompts of one adapter. Variants
replace the ledger by prose that carries the same facts, drop the
applicability fields, add or isolate a one-bit \texttt{applies} field
written by the reader, or let the judge also see the case.

\paragraph{Training.}
On rules the program supplies the target ledger $e^\ast(x,\rho,\omega)$ and,
for any ledger $e$, the verdict $y_\rho(e,s)$ obtained by executing the rule
on the state that $e$ describes. The reader is trained to write $e^\ast$.
The judge is trained on a ledger that is $e^\ast$ with probability $1-p$ and
otherwise the target ledger of another case of the same rule and condition,
always with the verdict that the ledger shown implies, so no instance
rewards a verdict that contradicts the evidence given to the judge
(objective in Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\paragraph{Symbolic gate.}
Three measurements (\S\ref{sec:results-ablation}) motivate a composite
system, \methodg: the applicability decision is what the pipeline learns,
the learned judge follows whole ledgers and not edits to single fields, and
a rule program applied to the predicted ledger matches the judge. \methodg{}
therefore computes whatever part of Eq.~\ref{eq:applic} is computable. Each
condition $k$ has a check $g_k(e,c)\in\{1,0,\bot\}$, with $\bot$ for not
formalised or not normalised: a string match for the quotation, a comparison
after unit conversion for a threshold, date arithmetic against a stated
reference date for a window, membership in the closure of a class once the
mention is linked to an ontology term by retrieval, and set membership for
subject and status. If no check returns $\bot$, the conjunction replaces the
reader's \texttt{applies} bit; otherwise the bit stands and the judge also
receives the case. With no criterion, or nothing to record, the system
abstains and falls back to a verdict-only score. A criterion never has to be
compiled in full: the gate acts on the parts that carry a number, a date or
a class (Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}).

\paragraph{Criterion grounding.}
Where the task states no rule, a \emph{grounding} function maps the task
identity $\tau$ (the name of a score, a registered criterion, a pair of
diagnoses, an option set) to a criterion text with a provenance tag,
$g(\tau)=(\rho,\pi)$, $\pi\in\{\text{stated},\text{derived},\text{self-written}\}$.
It never sees the case or its label, the members of a pair receive
identical text, and its source is frozen before any system is scored
(Appendix~\ref{app:ground}). Results are reported as a ladder over criterion
conditions: \emph{none}, \emph{stated} or \emph{derived},
\emph{self-written} by the frozen backbone from $\tau$ alone, and
\emph{wrong} (another item's criterion, or the pair's own with the two roles
exchanged), which tests that the verifier reads the rule.

For candidate selection, errors internal to a step are scored by a separate
step check, the released Med-PRM, and combined with the ledger score by the
minimum of the calibrated scores (Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Rules and shifts.}
The frozen library (\texttt{rule\_v1}) has \res{a15/rules_total@int} rules: \res{a15/rules_by_kind.score@int} scoring rules
implemented from published definitions, \res{d/a15/rules_total|a15/rules_by_kind.score|a15/rules_by_source.grammar_sampled@int} constraint rules compiled from
public recommendations by the tree-then-program procedure of
\citet{shen2026medguidex}, and \res{a15/rules_by_source.grammar_sampled@int} rules sampled from a typed grammar
(Appendix~\ref{app:rules}). Each rule has a structural signature (rule kind,
operators, dependency depth, input types, applicability predicate), in \res{a15/folds.1.n_classes@int}
classes. The main test, \textbf{L2}, has \res{a15/datasets.rule_v1/test_L2.n_groups} triplets from \res{a15/folds.1.n_l2_rules} rules of
held-out signature classes; L3-inv uses rules with invented names. Rendering
templates and cue phrases for relatives, negation and time are disjoint
between training and every test.

\paragraph{Training.}
All trained models use \bb{} with LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} of rank \res{meta/B-F-verdict-blocks/train.hp.lora_r} for one
epoch on \res{meta/B-F-verdict-blocks/train.examples} records. Four corpora differ only in the cases they
contain. \emph{Natural}: one case per group, the decisive condition rare.
\emph{Balanced}: one case per group, outcomes equally frequent.
\emph{Blocks}: the base case and the decisive edit of every group.
\emph{Triplets}: blocks in which a near-miss replaces the base case in half
of the groups. Representations: two one-stage formats (verdict only; the
ledger as a rationale followed by the verdict) and three two-stage formats
with a case-blind judge (a prose summary rendered from the same facts as the
ledger, a value ledger with \texttt{need} and \texttt{found} only, and the
applicability ledger).

\paragraph{Systems and protocol.}
We audit the released Med-PRM \citep{yun2025medprm}, MedS$^3$ PRM
\citep{jiang2025meds3} and FoVer PRM \citep{kamoi2026fover}, and on subsets
ThinkPRM-14B and GenPRM-7B. Training-free baselines on \bb{} are the
zero-shot critic, default correction \citep{shi2024cad}, a prompted summary,
a prompted ledger, and a verifier that writes and executes a program for the
rule \citep{ocampo2026solver}; larger open judges are in Table~\ref{tab:openjudges}, closed ones \ph{not yet scored}
(Appendix~\ref{app:more}). Scores are verdict log-odds. Key cells have five
seeds; other values are from seed 0. Paired differences use a cluster
bootstrap over rules (patients for TrialGPT, label pairs for MedEinst). The
analysis plan was fixed before the test sets were scored, and a second
stage was registered after its outcome and before any of its data were built
(Appendix~\ref{app:plan}).

\subsection{What the judge uses}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

\emph{One applicability bit carries the result.} If the reader also writes
whether the condition applies, a judge that sees only that bit and the
claim solves \res{run/B-AB-bitonly-judge/L2/all/TA/s0}\% of L2 triplets, and a reader trained to write the bit
and nothing else 99.95\% (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Both reach \res{run/B-AB-bitonly-judge/xr_v1:test/xa/XA/s0}\%

what is learned is the reader's decision that a finding counts under the
rule in view. On rule-side items that differ in a time window the bit
reader solves \res{run/B-AB-bitonly-reader/xr_v1:test/xa/XA_window/s0@0}\% and the ledger \res{run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/xr_v1:test/xa/XA_window/s0@0}\%, because a field that says
``current'' or ``past'' cannot carry a date.

\emph{The learned judge follows whole ledgers, not fields.} Given the ledger
of another case of the same rule and condition, the judge returns the
verdict that ledger implies in 99.9\% of instances. When one of
\texttt{subject}, \texttt{status} or \texttt{time} is edited in a correct
ledger, it follows \res{json/results_git/B-AE-field-edit/summary_rule_v1~test_L2~edit.json::by.changed=1.agreement}\% of the edits that should change the verdict and
keeps \res{json/results_git/B-AE-field-edit/summary_rule_v1~test_L2~edit.json::by.changed=0.agreement}\% of the verdicts that should stay: it is not a faithful function
of the fields. A rule program applied to the predicted
ledger gives \res{run/B-AE-program-ledger/L2/all/TA}\%, against \res{run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/L2/all/TA/s0}\% for the learned judge ($+\res{d/run/B-AE-program-ledger/L2/all/TA|run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/L2/all/TA/s0}$
$[0.0,0.6]$). Where a rule is executable, what the pipeline contributes is
the reading; hence the gate of \S\ref{sec:ledger}.

\emph{Conditions not seen in training.} Trained on triplets without one kind
of near-miss, the verdict-only model solves 7.0\% of L2 triplets of the kind
``another person'' (\res{run/B-F-verdict-triplets/L2/nm_kind=subject/TA}\% when trained with it), 60.2\% for ``past''
(\res{run/B-F-verdict-triplets/L2/nm_kind=time/TA}\%) and 49.8\% for ``threshold'' (\res{run/B-F-verdict-triplets/L2/nm_kind=boundary/TA}\%); the ledger \res{run/B-LOKO-subject-ledger2/L2/nm_kind=subject/TA}\%, \res{run/B-LOKO-time-ledger2/L2/nm_kind=time/TA}\%
and \res{run/B-LOKO-boundary-ledger2/L2/nm_kind=boundary/TA}\%
(Table~\ref{tab:loko}). Near-miss supervision has to cover each condition
of Eq.~\ref{eq:applic}, which is the reason to hand a condition to a

\subsection{Reward use: selection and policy training}
\label{sec:results-downstream}

\paragraph{Selection.}
Reranking \res{run/D-SEL-combined/sel:medqa/top/N@int} samples of a frozen policy on exam questions tests the verifier
as a process reward where no criterion is stated
(Table~\ref{tab:downstream}). The expectation of the first plan, that
adding the ledger score to a step check improves on the step check, is not
supported ($\res{d/run/D-SEL-combined/sel:medqa/top/acc|run/D-SEL-stepcheck/sel:medqa/top/acc}$ points on MedQA $[\res{cmp/sel-mqa-comb-step/lo},\res{cmp/sel-mqa-comb-step/hi}]$), and self-consistency is the
best selector. As a trace score for answer correctness the ledger score has
an AUC of 0.51 (Med-PRM: 0.65): with no criterion in the input it has
nothing to verify.

\paragraph{Policy training.}
A process reward can be optimised without solving the task, whether or not
it passes the triplet test. We train a Qwen3.5-4B policy with GRPO for \res{meta/D-RL-outcome/steps} steps on rule tasks
and evaluate it with the rule program on \res{run/D-RL-outcome/L2/top/n_triplets/s0@int} held-out L2 pairs (seed 0;
descriptive). With an outcome reward, accuracy on both members of a pair
rises from \res{run/D-RL-outcome/L2/start/pair/s0}\% to \res{run/D-RL-outcome/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\%, and with a reference-graph reward after
\citet{medceg2025} to \res{run/D-RL-refgraph/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\%. With the released Med-PRM as the reward it
falls from \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/start/pair/s0}\% to \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\% while the mean reward reaches \res{run/D-RL-stepcheck/L2/step=1000/reward/s0@4}; within a
rollout group this reward is uncorrelated with correctness ($-0.05$). The two

(to \res{run/D-RL-ledger2-blocks/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\% and \res{run/D-RL-ledger2-triplets/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\%). The
collapsed policies write one short step and answer; most errors are
failures to hold (all runs but one; Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\section{Conclusion}

On executable rules, medical reward models fail in two directions, and
supervision on decisive evidence alone cures the first failure and produces
the second. Near-misses in the same budget remove it; one learned bit,
whether the finding counts under the rule, accounts for the gain.
Such a verifier follows edits to the rule and is unchanged on benchmarks
that state none: on the rule tier, selectivity is relative to the stated
rule. On clinical notes with a published score, near-misses help again, but
not demonstrably by reading the criterion.

\section*{Limitations}

\textbf{What the labels are.} Rule-tier labels establish compliance with a
stated rule, its applicability predicates and a rendering grammar. They say
what the rule implies, not what is clinically optimal, and that a program
produced a label does not show that the text expresses the state, that the
test is free of generator artefacts, or that the behaviour matters outside
the generator (Appendix~\ref{app:rules}). We commissioned no clinical
annotation, no clinician reviewed the rules, and the reading of rendered
groups before the freeze was done by model sessions, not by people; reused
benchmarks carry the review their authors report. The TrialGPT annotations
are physician judgments on synthetic patient summaries, made with a
model's explanation in view; MedEinst cases derive from a
simulator and were edited and filtered by models, with a physician review of
a sample reported by its authors; key pairs inherit the errors of exam keys
and are not counterfactuals. Nothing here supports clinical use.

\textbf{Transfer.} The verifiers trained on rules do not improve agreement
with any clinical benchmark that leaves its criterion implicit, and the
improvement where a criterion is stated is not statistically established.
On clinical notes with a stated score definition the gain from near-misses
is established for items of \res{sum/A-SETS/mcv_v1_accept.n_accepted@int} clinical scores; the part of it that is due to
reading the criterion is not, and the comparisons on class conditions, a
derived criterion and the composite are not yet run.

\textbf{Sources of criteria.} A criterion derived from a knowledge base or
an ontology is exact only relative to that source and its release. Open
knowledge bases mostly list associations, not decisions; class membership
differs between drug classifications; we take one source, the FDA
established pharmacologic classes, and report separately the classes on
which a second source agrees, and a missing contraindication assertion is not evidence of
safety. Giving a model the lists from which a benchmark was generated
changes what the benchmark measures, which is why the ladder always includes
the condition without a criterion and the untrained backbone under the same
text. Self-written criteria can be wrong, and a rule-follower follows wrong
rules.

\textbf{The rule language.} The test is generated from a finite language of
applicability conditions. Held-out signature classes reuse primitives and
operators seen in training, \res{a15/folds.1.overlap_train_vs_L2.criterion_subexpressions}\% of criterion subexpressions of the main
test also occur in training rules, there are few such classes, and most
judgments concern one condition of one rule. A trigger lexicon that is given
the test cue phrases solves the generated test, and near-miss supervision
does not extend to a condition it has not covered
(\S\ref{sec:results-ablation}). The frozen library has no time windows and
no class-level concepts.

\textbf{The reader.} The record assumes that a claim depends on a few
identifiable findings. Claims that weigh many soft findings fit it poorly:
on long diagnostic narratives over a third of the reader's outputs are
malformed.
A quotation can exist in the case and still be the wrong evidence, the
judge is not a faithful function of the fields, and on the rule tier the
condition under test is supplied.

\textbf{Verifier, process reward, training reward.} The triplet results
concern a claim verifier. As a selector on exam questions the ledger score
is uninformative. The policy experiment has one policy and at most three seeds
per reward. Our own process rewards are exploited as well: with the ledger
score as the only reward, pair accuracy falls from \res{run/D-RL-ledger2-triplets/L2/top/start_pair/s0}\% to
\res{run/D-RL-ledger2-triplets/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\% (trained on triplets) and to \res{run/D-RL-ledger2-blocks/L2/top/acc_pair/s0}\% (trained on
blocks). A verifier that passes the triplet test is therefore not a safe
training reward by itself.

\textbf{Scope.} One backbone family; five seeds for the key cells and one
elsewhere; no closed judge has been scored; English only.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}

The work uses public benchmarks and generated cases; no patient data were
collected. Stated rules in our data are test specifications and must not be
read as prescribing guidance, and criteria derived from knowledge bases or
written by a model are not clinical recommendations. A reward model that
appears to check patient evidence could be trusted more than it should be;
accuracy on these triplets does not establish safety in care, and the models
are released for research on post-training only. Because the engine
generates cases on which a given reward fails, it could be used to construct
inputs that mislead a deployed evaluator; we see this as an argument for
auditing rewards before they are used. Redistributed items derived from
MedCalc-Bench carry its share-alike licence, and ontology content is used
under the terms of each source (Appendix~\ref{app:ground}).

\section{Triplet Construction}
\label{app:twins}

\paragraph{Invariants.}
Every edit is checked on the target claim by executing the program: a flip
must change the claim, a near-miss and a presentation edit must not, also
for rules that combine criteria. A re-execution of this check over all
frozen sets found no violation in \res{stats/target_claim_check.groups_total} groups (\res{stats/target_claim_check.records_total} records). Of
the proposed groups of the triplet corpus, \res{a15/datasets.rule_v1/train_triplets.reject_rate}\% (\res{stats/rejects.logged.rule_v1/train_triplets.rejected} of \res{stats/rejects.logged.rule_v1/train_triplets.proposed}) were
rejected, all for a presentation edit; none of the \res{stats/rejects.logged.rule_v1/test_L2.proposed} proposed L2
groups was. Each run trains on 60{,}000 examples; L2 has 2{,}000
triplets and the development set \res{a15/datasets.rule_v1/dev.n_groups}. No evaluation input contains the edit
type, the role of the case, or the state.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:blind}
Let $u$ be deterministic. (i) If $u(s,x)=g(s)$, then $d(x)=d(\xf)$. (ii) If
$u(s,x)=g(x)$, then $d\equiv 0$. (iii) If $u(s,x)=g(s,h(x))$ for a feature map
$h$ with $h(\xf)=h(\xn)$, then $d(\xf)=d(\xn)$. In each case the triplet is
not solved; under (iii) a correct reversal on the flip implies failure on the
near-miss.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} (i) and (ii) are immediate from the definition of $d$.
(iii) $d(\xf)=g(s,h(\xf))-g(s',h(\xf))=g(s,h(\xn))-g(s',h(\xn))=d(\xn)$, while
a solved triplet needs $d(\xf)<0<d(\xn)$. \hfill$\square$

\smallskip
Let $h_k(x)\in\{0,1\}$ indicate whether the decisive concept $k$ is named
anywhere in the case, whatever its value, subject, status or time. For a
finding that the flip introduces, the base case is rendered without naming
$k$ and an applicability near-miss names it, so $h_k(x)=0$ and
$h_k(\xf)=h_k(\xn)=1$: (iii) applies. For a measured input $k$ is named in
all three cases, so $d(x)=d(\xf)$ as in (i). A scorer that uses the case only
through $h_k$ thus solves no triplet, although it can reverse on every flip
of the first kind (\res{pct/stats/semantics.test_L2.finding_bases|a15/datasets.rule_v1/test_L2.n_groups@0}\% of L2 flips). The proposition is a check on the
construction and does not exclude other shortcuts, and it does not extend to
sampled decisions.

\paragraph{Semantics.}
\emph{Several mentions.} A criterion uses the mentions its predicate counts;
for a measurement \ph{the current value}, for a finding any counted mention
with status present. \emph{Numbers.} Thresholds are inclusive or exclusive
as the rule text says, and values exactly at a threshold form their own
near-miss kind. \emph{Time.} In \texttt{rule\_v1} a mention is current or
past, and a past mention carries a year; there is no reference date and no
rule with a time window. Windows occur only in the rule-side items and, with
a stated reference date and a stated treatment of the boundary day, in the
stage-2 sets. \emph{Omission.} Measurements that are not given are unknown;
history items that are not mentioned are absent, unless the case says they
are unknown. This is a stipulation of the generator, not a general reading
of clinical notes. External datasets keep their own conventions; \mcv, for
example, scores an unmentioned finding as absent, with documented
exceptions.

\paragraph{Missing twins.}
We distinguish explicit absence, which determines a criterion, from an
unknown input. With $C_\rho(z')$ the non-empty set of admissible completions
of a partial state $z'$, a claim $s$ is \emph{supported} if every completion
supports it, \emph{refuted} if every completion refutes it, and
\emph{undetermined} otherwise; inconsistent states are excluded. A missing
twin is kept only if the target claim is undetermined, which an unknown
criterion does not by itself guarantee. A verdict ``$+$'' means supported;
``$-$'' covers refuted and undetermined claims, which verifiers for retrieval
keep apart \citep{qiu2026surerag,kamelhar2026gsar}. MR is the rate at which
both claims of a missing twin are rejected at the threshold that gives 5\%
false rejection of supported claims on development data. It is \res{run/C-AUD-medprm/missing/top/MR}\% for
Med-PRM, \res{run/C-AUD-meds3/missing/top/MR}\% for the MedS$^3$ PRM, \res{run/C-AUD-fover/missing/top/MR}\% for the FoVer PRM, \res{run/C-TF-critic/missing/top/MR}\% for the
untrained critic and \res{run/C-TF-promptsum/missing/top/MR}\% for the prompted summary, and between \res{run/B-F-rationale-natural/missing/top/MR}\% and
\res{run/B-F-verdict-blocks/missing/top/MR/max}\% for every trained model: the marker of a missing input is learnable
from the 15\% of such records in each corpus, so we report this as a limit of
the test and not as evidence of abstention.

\paragraph{Harder set.}
The harder set is built on the L2 rules and adds long notes with unrelated
lines, superseded values (an older value crosses the threshold, the latest
does not) and relabelled lines; counts per requirement are \tbd.

\paragraph{Rule-side items.}
For a fixed case, two rules differ only in the applicability clause: time
window, currency, subject, or boundary inclusivity (\res{json/data/xr_v1/test/MANIFEST.json::by_dimension.window} items each; \res{json/data/xr_v1/test/MANIFEST.json::n_records}
records). Each item has three cases (the contested form, an uncontested
positive, an absent finding) and is solved only if all six judgments are
right. Scorers that ignore the rule text, never count the contested form or
always count it solve no item; the rule program solves all, and uniformly

\paragraph{\mcv.}
MedCalc-Bench Verified, tag v1.0.8, CC BY-SA 4.0. The test file has 1{,}100
rows for 55 calculators; the 19 rule-based ones (categories risk, severity
and diagnosis) have 380 rows, of which 230 have notes extracted from
published text (186 PMC case reports, 42 USMLE vignettes, 2 TREC topics) and
150 have notes written by a model and edited or approved by a clinician.
The training file has 2{,}426 rule-based rows (1{,}669 extracted); the
original benchmark paper states that the extractions of its training set
were not verified by hand, and we treat them as silver. Eight scores have only extracted test notes
(CHA$_2$DS$_2$-VASc, CURB-65, Centor, Charlson, FeverPAIN,
Glasgow-Blatchford, SIRS, Wells for pulmonary embolism). Each row gives the
text of the score, a dictionary of entity values, and the answer.
\emph{Rule code:} we implement each score from its stated text; a score
enters the tier only if our code reproduces the released answer on every
row from the released entities (\res{sum/A-SETS/mcv_v1_accept.n_accepted@int} of 19 scores, \res{sum/A-SETS/mcv_v1_accept.test_rows_covered@int} rows).
\emph{Items.} (A) Criterion claims on untouched notes, in three strata:
value stated in the note, finding explicitly denied, entity absent and
settled by the benchmark's default. (B) The numeric values that lie within a
narrow band of a threshold without any edit. (C) Single-value edits: for a
numeric criterion whose value string occurs exactly once in the note and
feeds no other criterion, the value is replaced by one across the threshold
(flip) or by one on the same side and nearer to it (near-miss); labels are
recomputed by rule code. (D) One-sentence insertions for a criterion that is
not met: the qualifying finding attributed to a relative, negated, or, only
for criteria that require a current state, placed in the past (near-misses),
and the same finding affirmed for the patient (flip). \emph{What is and is
not exact.} Labels are exact given the entity dictionary; the dictionary is
released annotation and can be wrong. Of the 62 rule-based test rows whose
note also occurs, unchanged, in the independently corrected labels of
\citet{ye2025stewardship}, that set leaves 10 without an answer and gives a
different answer for 9 of the other 52. Edits of types C and D compare cases within one note, so an error
in an unrelated entity cancels for criterion claims. Inserted sentences are
written by us, as in the rule tier, now inside real notes. History-type
criteria (a prior stroke in CHA$_2$DS$_2$-VASc) count past findings, so a
past mention there is a flip, not a near-miss. \emph{Adaptation.} Verifiers
are also fine-tuned on items built from the training notes, with and without
near-miss edits at equal budget, with the scores chosen by the seeded rule of the plan
(Appendix~\ref{app:plan}) held out entirely for test.

\paragraph{Registered criteria.}
Chia \citep{kury2020chia} annotates 12{,}409 criteria of 1{,}000 trials with
entities, values, temporal constraints, negation and Boolean scope
(inter-annotator $\kappa$ of 0.80 at phrase level), and its authors state
that an annotation graph is meant to be read as a Boolean expression over
patient data; the Leaf Clinical Trials corpus \citep{dobbins2022lct}
annotates 1{,}006 trial descriptions with comparison operators, values,
units, temporal periods, negation and family members. Both are CC BY 4.0. We
keep single-condition criteria that carry a numeric threshold or a time
window and none of the corpora's flags for criteria that cannot be
represented or need subjective judgment (\res{sum/A-SETS/reg_v1.selection.LCT.candidates@int} in the Leaf corpus and
\res{sum/A-SETS/reg_v1.selection.Chia.candidates@int} in Chia), and compile the annotation to a program
by script. The annotation fixes structure, not execution semantics: whether
``within six months'' includes the boundary day, and what the reference date
is, remain our stated conventions and are printed in the prompt. Cases are
generated as on the rule tier, with dates. Trials and criterion texts that
occur in the TrialGPT annotations are excluded. An earlier set of \res{sum/A-SETS/ec_v1_prepare.kept}
criteria from \res{sum/A-SETS/ec_v1_prepare.trials} trials that we formalised ourselves is not scored: its
programs were never signed off by two authors.

\paragraph{Knowledge-base criteria.}
The public part of the DDXPlus knowledge base is two files: for each of 49
conditions the set of associated symptoms and antecedents (888 links, 605 to
symptoms and 283 to antecedents), and for each of 223 findings (110
symptoms, 113 antecedents; 208 binary, 10 categorical, 5 multiple-choice)
its question text in English. No probabilities are released. For a pair of
diagnoses $(A,B)$ a script renders, from the question texts and in a
canonical order of the two names, the findings listed for $A$ only, for $B$
only and for both, followed by the procedure: \emph{a finding listed for
one diagnosis only excludes the other; if findings of both exclusive lists
are present, or of neither, the criterion does not decide}. The script and
the hashes of the two files are committed before any model is run. Because,
by its authors' description, the same knowledge base guided the construction
of MedEinst's trap cases, handing the lists to a model changes what that
benchmark measures; the claim we test is the
contrast between trained and untrained verifiers under identical text.
\emph{Controls:} a symbolic executor that maps narratives to findings and
applies the procedure, run first; a string-match baseline that counts hits
in the two exclusive lists; every system with and without the criterion;
the criterion of an unrelated pair; the pair's own criterion with the two
lists exchanged; the two diagnosis names replaced by neutral labels; lists
without the procedure. \emph{Knowledge-base triplets:} for a DDXPlus test
patient with diagnosis $A$ and a second diagnosis $B$, a script removes the
findings listed for $A$ only and adds findings listed for $B$ only (flip),
or adds a $B$-only finding in a form the stated criterion does not count
(near-miss); labels follow from the procedure. The lists themselves contain
findings about relatives and about the past, which count where listed, so a
verifier that has learned ``family means ignore'' fails here by design.

\paragraph{Class membership from open ontologies.}
Table~\ref{tab:onto} lists the sources. A class-level criterion is grounded
to a class identifier and a release. \emph{Membership} is the is-a closure
in HPO and Mondo; for drugs it is defined at the level of ingredients: a
mention is normalised to an RxNorm ingredient, and it belongs to a class if
it is a direct ingredient member of an FDA established pharmacologic class
served by RxClass. We first required agreement with an ATC fourth-level
class; that rule left 28 drug classes, 7 of them for test, and was replaced
before the class set was built (Appendix~\ref{app:plan}). Classes on which
both sources agree are marked, and results are also reported on them. \emph{Negatives} for the near-miss must
pass an assumed-disjointness test in the sense of \citet{he2023ontolama}:
no subsumption in either direction and no common descendant; combination
products and ingredients with several class memberships are excluded.
\emph{Class triplets:} base, no member mentioned; flip, a member, under its
ingredient, brand or synonym name; near-miss, a relative outside the
closure, or a term too general to establish membership. Crossing with the
other conditions (a relative's lisinopril, lisinopril stopped last year) is
free. \emph{Linking} at inference is retrieval over labels and synonyms of
the release followed by a choice among the retrieved candidates by the
reader, never free generation of identifiers; its accuracy is reported
separately from the verifier's. Two regimes are kept apart: \emph{open
book}, in which the member list or the gate is available, and \emph{closed
book}, in which the model must know the class. Labels are exact relative to
the release and the membership rule above, both stated with every item.
Contraindication assertions (MED-RT, via RxClass) are used only in the
positive direction: a missing assertion is not evidence of safety.

\paragraph{Self-written criteria and controls.}
For key pairs the frozen backbone writes, from the option set and without
the vignette, what would have to hold for each of the two answers; the text
is given identically to every system, a mismatched text from another pair
is the control, and both rows are reported with the row without a
criterion. For every tier the ladder is reported per system with the
untrained backbone alongside, so that an effect of the added text on any
model is not mistaken for an effect of training. A rule-side invariance arm
complements the rule-side items: the rule text is rewritten without changing
its program (clause order, placement of the exception, synonyms), which
executing both programs certifies, and the excess rate of changed verdicts
over a rerun baseline is reported \citep{weng2026policy,yang2026compared}
(\tbd).

\section{Ledger Format, Reader Statistics and Gate}
\label{app:ledger}

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}

\noindent Given this ledger, the rule and the claim ``Order amoxicillin'', the
judge returns \texttt{+}. For the main model, malformed ledgers occur in at most 0.2\% of
rule-tier reader outputs, in 1.9--\res{run/C-TG-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:trialgpt_test/eval/malformed_rate/max@pct1}\% on TrialGPT, \res{pct/run/C-KP-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:keypairs_medqa_oneway/eval/malformed_units/s0|run/C-KP-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:keypairs_medqa_oneway/eval/reader_units/s0}--\res{pct/run/C-KP-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:keypairs_careqa_oneway/eval/malformed_units/s0|run/C-KP-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:keypairs_careqa_oneway/eval/reader_units/s0}\% on key
pairs and \res{pct/run/C-ME-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:medeinst_test/eval/malformed_units/s0|run/C-ME-ledger2-triplets/clin_v1:medeinst_test/eval/reader_units/s0}\% on MedEinst: the format degrades as the input moves away
from a stated rule and a short case. A malformed ledger is recorded as an
invalid outcome and both claims are rejected. Neither record format may
state a verdict: no claim sentence and no decision phrase occurs in the
reader outputs of any of the 27 two-stage runs (for example, in none of
37{,}497 summaries of one run). In 30.2\% of judge training examples the
ledger shown is a donor's. A verification pass that re-reads every entry
against the case and regenerates rejected ones once adds \res{d/run/B-AB-verify/L2/all/TA/s0|run/B-F-ledger2-triplets/L2/all/TA/s0} points
($[0.1,1.2]$, $p=0.012$). In triplet and pair evaluations one ledger serves
both claims.

\paragraph{Parts and whole.}
For each base--flip pair we build a \emph{reading} pair, whose claims quote
the decisive finding, and an \emph{application} pair, which scores the
original claims against a one-line case with only that finding. With $P$,
$K$, $U$ the events of a correct reversal on the reading pair, the
application pair and the full pair, the composition gap is
$G=1-\Pr[U\mid P\wedge K]$; it is descriptive, since the tasks differ in
length and in what must be selected. For the untrained backbone on \res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.n@int}
pairs, $P=\res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.P}\%$, $K=\res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.K}\%$, $U=\res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.U}\%$, and $G=\res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.G}\%$ among the \res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.n_P_and_K@int} pairs
with $P$ and $K$. On L0 its base accuracy is \res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.L0_BaseAcc}\% and, among triplets with
a correct base preference, it reverses on the near-miss in \res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.L0_UR}\% ($n=\res{json/results_git/C-TF-critic/diagnostics.json::pk.L0_UR_n@int}$).

\paragraph{Shift relative to a missing-input baseline.}
Let $d_0=d(\xm)$, $\delta(x)=d(x)-d_0$, and $y\in\{+1,-1\}$ the correct sign
of $d(x)$. For cases with $y\,d_0<0$, where the baseline preference opposes
the label, the preference is right if and only if $y\,\delta(x)>|d_0|$. We
sort these cases into four disjoint classes, in this order: \emph{crossed}
(the inequality holds); \emph{unmoved} ($|\delta|\le\tau$, the model's median
$|d(x^{\mathrm{p}})-d(x)|$ over presentation edits); \emph{wrong direction}
($y\delta<0$); \emph{short}. The shift ratio $\kappa$ divides the median of
$y\,\delta(x)$ over these cases by the model's median response to an error
inside a step \ph{(state the pairs used for the denominator; the planned
source, MedPRMBench, is not released)}. Untrained backbone: \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-qwen35-9b/crossed}\% crossed,
\res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-qwen35-9b/short}\% short, \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-qwen35-9b/unmoved}\% unmoved, \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-qwen35-9b/wrong}\% wrong direction, $\kappa=\res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-qwen35-9b/kappa@2}$.
Med-PRM: \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-medprm/crossed}\% crossed, \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-medprm/short}\% short, \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-medprm/unmoved}\% unmoved, $\kappa=\res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=C-AUD-medprm/kappa@2}$.
Verdict only trained on blocks: \res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=B-F-verdict-blocks/crossed}\% crossed, $\kappa=\res{run/C-DG-shift/L0/signal=B-F-verdict-blocks/kappa@2}$. Med-PRM's
preference moves in the right direction and stops short in two of five
conflicting cases.

\paragraph{Objective.}
Let $N(x)$ be the other training cases of the same rule and condition as
$x$, including its twins, and let $q(\cdot\mid x,\rho,\omega)$ put mass
$1-p$ on $e^\ast(x,\rho,\omega)$ and spread $p$ uniformly over
$\{e^\ast(x',\rho,\omega): x'\in N(x)\}$. Then
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{(x,\rho,s)}\Bigl[&-\tfrac{1}{|e^\ast|}\log
p_\phi(e^\ast\mid x,\rho,\omega)\nonumber\\[-2pt]
&-\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{e\sim q}\log p_\phi\bigl(y_\rho(e,s)\mid e,\rho,s\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{align}
with $|e^\ast|$ the number of target tokens. Donor ledgers are gold ledgers,
whereas at test time the judge reads generated ones; the program-supplied
ledger analysis measures that gap.

\paragraph{Training.}
LoRA rank \res{meta/B-F-ledger2-triplets/train.hp.lora_r@0}, batch size \res{json/results_git/B-F-ledger2-triplets-s0/meta.json::train.hp.batch@int}, one epoch over \res{json/results_git/B-F-ledger2-triplets-s0/meta.json::train.examples@int} records, learning rate
\ph{1e-4}, $\lambda=\ph{1.0}$, donor probability $p=0.3$ (a donor's ledger
in 30.2\% of judge examples). The two loss terms are separate supervised
examples in one corpus: one reader example per case and condition, shared by
both claims, and one judge example per claim. A deterministic parser maps
ledger text to a state for $y_\rho$; incomplete or inconsistent ledgers have
no state and yield no judge example. A verdict-only run takes about 2.2
GPU-hours and a two-stage run about 2.8, including evaluation. Unique cases,
reader and judge targets, tokens and optimiser steps per configuration are
\tbd; equal record counts do not by themselves mean equal supervision.
Scores from the served model used for selection agree in sign with those of

\section{Extended Related Work}
\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Validity of evaluators.}
\citet{chen2026judge} define a judge's validity as invariance to
construct-preserving edits together with sensitivity to construct-changing
ones, and \citet{weng2026policy} hold the judged trajectory fixed and
rewrite the policy given to the judge. \citet{yang2026compared} show that
the effect of a targeted counterfactual edit must be compared with that of
meaning-preserving edits of the same size; our near-miss and presentation
edits are such baselines with known labels. Robustness work on reward models
targets response-side artifacts
\citep{liu2025rrm,zang2025auditor,xu2025contextualjudge}. In medicine,
answer-changing patient facts \citep{hou2026medpic,chen2026medeinst,kim2025marc}
and clinically irrelevant distractor statements
\citep{vishwanath2025distracted} have each been tested on answering models; none of these puts a decisive edit and
an inapplicable mention of the same concept in one item, varies the
applicability clause of a rule with the case fixed, or applies patient-side
edits to released medical PRMs.

\paragraph{Supervision with and without label-preserving edits.}
Minimal label-flipping edits serve training \citep{kaushik2020cad} and
evaluation
\citep{gardner2020contrast,ribeiro2020checklist,thrush2022winoground}, with
the caveat that they teach which feature matters and not when it does not
\citep{joshi2022cad}. Adding label-preserving edits of the same feature
removes over-triggering for negation scope \citep{she2023scone} and for
safety policies: a guard model trained on label-changing policy edits alone
reaches precision .50 at recall 1.00, and .90 and .78 with both kinds
\citep{liu2026apt}. Natural text remains hard after such training
\citep{ravichander2022condaqa}. CROME trains reward models with
augmentations that change causal attributes of a response and with neutral
ones \citep{srivastava2026crome}; DynaCF down-weights pairs whose margin
shifts under content-preserving rewrites \citep{liu2026dynacf}, which we
adapt to case edits as an alternative to training on near-misses; CSR
regularises a solver to change its answer when an operator in its own trace
is swapped \citep{shihab2025csr}. MedGuideX post-trains a clinical policy on
factual and counterfactual questions from executable guideline functions
and keeps only interventions that change the outcome \citep{shen2026medguidex}.
Sensitivity together with invariance is therefore established as a principle.
Our negatives differ in kind: they keep the decisive concept in the text and
remove one condition of its applicability, a case that \citet{winston1970}
called a near miss, and we measure what a verifier learns without them.

\paragraph{Process rewards and symbolic verification.}
PRMs are trained on human step labels \citep{lightman2024verify}, rollout
estimates \citep{wang2024mathshepherd} or generated verification reasoning
\citep{khalifa2025thinkprm}, which GenPRM extends with executed code
\citep{zhao2025genprm}. FoVer trains step verifiers on labels from formal
tools and shows transfer to informal tasks \citep{kamoi2026fover};
generating supervision by formal verification is established and is not our
claim. NS-PRM separates what a deterministic verifier can decide
(schema, arithmetic, units) from what a PRM must learn, trains the PRM on
negatives that pass the verifier, and searches verifier-first
\citep{zi2026nsprm}; its perturbations all change the label. We adopt the
division of labour and add the label-preserving side. Other work injects
errors into verified chains \citep{chi2026vcs} or uses a deterministic
verifier as the reward \citep{pronesti2026vprm}. In medicine, Med-PRM and
Med-TIV condition a verifier on retrieved passages
\citep{yun2025medprm,zhang2026medtiv}, MedCEG rewards coverage of a
reference evidence graph \citep{medceg2025}, and MedPRMBench injects 14
error types into chains \citep{wu2026medprmbench}. Scoring from a record
written in a separate pass follows Chain-of-Verification
\citep{dhuliawala2024cove} and EVPV, which extracts premises once per
problem and attenuates the reward of steps they do not support
\citep{evpv2026}; mediating a reward through explicit concepts precedes our
record \citep{koh2020cbm,laguna2025cbrm}. Its fields are the attributes that
clinical NLP assigns to mentions: negation, experiencer and temporality
\citep{chapman2001negex,harkema2009context,uzuner2011i2b2}. For composite
neuro-symbolic systems in medicine, \citet{wong2026nesy} name the failure we
measure at the reader: an extraction error never activates the rule layer.

\paragraph{Stated rules and where criteria come from.}
Reasoning from a stated rule and a described case has a long line of
benchmarks \citep{saeidi2018sharc,holzenberger2020sara,clark2020ruletaker,sun2024rulebench},
and policy-conditioned guard models are trained to follow rules given at
inference \citep{hoover2025dynaguard,liu2026apt}. Whether a verdict depends
on the rule has to be tested: deleting, shuffling or replacing the rule left
the accuracy of every compliance detector audited by
\citet{sadhu2026compliance} unchanged. For clinical criteria, executable
guideline functions are called at inference \citep{cao2026guideskill},
diagnostic hypotheses are checked against guideline-derived disease profiles
\citep{cao2026veridx}, conditional QA gates answers on a stated patient
condition \citep{parekh2026cgr}, and trial eligibility is decided criterion
by criterion by prompted models \citep{jin2024trialgpt} or by an SMT solver
over compiled criteria and typed patient facts, with an audit of whether
changing pivotal facts changes the decision \citep{zhou2026verdict}. As far as
we could determine, these systems prompt their reader, and none trains it on
inapplicable evidence.
Ontologies have supplied exactly labelled subsumption data with sibling
negatives \citep{he2023ontolama,he2024hit} and sibling substitutions that a
clinical verifier must not accept \citep{qu2026medieval}; injecting an
ontology into weights changed medical QA little \citep{liu2025ontotune},
whereas a fixed type vocabulary used as a gate before a learned judge helped
knowledge-graph QA \citep{shan2026ontgqa}. We use ontologies in the second
way, and pair a class member that must flip a verdict with a non-member
that must not.

\paragraph{Context against prior, and reward exploitation.}
How language models arbitrate between memory and conflicting context is well
studied \citep{xu2024conflicts,wu2024clasheval,shi2024cad}, and
clinical-trial NLI has been probed with label-preserving and label-inverting
interventions \citep{jullien2024semeval}; claim-only scorers are
partial-input baselines \citep{poliak2018hypothesis}. Policies trained
against learned rewards exploit them
\citep{skalse2022hacking,gao2023scaling,helff2026gaming}: against math PRMs
the reward approaches its maximum while accuracy stays below 4\%
\citep{tiwari2026reward}, rubric and claim-checker rewards in medicine are
exploited as training proceeds \citep{mahmoud2026hacking,ji2026checker}, and Med-PRM as a process reward
gave lower accuracy than an outcome reward in risk-of-bias assessment
\citep{pronesti2026vprm}. Our policy experiment adds a held-out, exactly
labelled evaluation and connects the exploited reward to its triplet
profile.

\paragraph{What the frozen generator changed.}
\texttt{rule\_v1} holds out signature classes, templates and cue phrases;

\paragraph{What the results changed.}
Three expectations of the first plan were wrong and are reported as such:
that balanced outcomes would teach reversal (\S\ref{sec:results-factorial}),
that the ledger and the step check would combine into a better selector
(\S\ref{sec:results-downstream}), and that training on rules would transfer
without a stated criterion (\S\ref{sec:results-shift}). The typed ledger,
which the first plan treated as the method, turned out to matter less than
the supervision and to be replaceable by one bit. Stage 2 is the plan that
follows from these outcomes.

\paragraph{Errors of the trained systems.}
Sheets with 200 failures of five trained systems on L2 (175 distinct
triplets), to be coded by the authors as extraction error, applicability
error, unseen wording, rule misread or other, are prepared; the coding is
\ph{pending}.
